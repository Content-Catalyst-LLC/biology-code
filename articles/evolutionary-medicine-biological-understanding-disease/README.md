# Evolutionary Medicine and the Biological Understanding of Disease

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

Evolutionary Medicine and the Biological Understanding of Disease

The repository provides reproducible computational scaffolding for evolutionary medicine. It includes synthetic antimicrobial-resistance scenarios, mismatch scoring, life-history allocation tables, somatic-evolution models, defense-threshold examples, SQL provenance structures, notebook scaffolding, and cross-language examples in Python, R, Julia, Fortran, Rust, Go, C, and C++.

No university affiliation is implied by this repository. The design goal is advanced reproducible computational rigor: clear assumptions, transparent disease-evolution scenarios, interpretable mathematical models, provenance records, responsible-use notes, and reusable scientific workflow structure.

## Repository Structure

- python/ — resistance selection, mismatch scoring, life-history trade-offs, clonal expansion, defense thresholds, provenance, and reporting
- r/ — life-history allocation and somatic-evolution cross-checks
- julia/ — evolutionary medicine numerical kernels
- fortran/ — legacy-style resistant-frequency kernel
- rust/ — safe command-line evolutionary scenario ranking utility
- go/ — portable disease-scenario metadata summary helper
- c/ — compact selection model kernel
- cpp/ — evolutionary disease scenario ranking example
- sql/ — disease scenarios, evolutionary mechanisms, resistance scenarios, mismatch exposures, provenance, and notes
- docs/ — setup, methodology, validation, responsible use, and reproducibility notes
- data/ — synthetic evolutionary medicine datasets
- notebooks/ — minimal notebook scaffold
- outputs/ — generated tables, reports, manifests, and figures

## Scientific Focus

The workflows demonstrate:

1. Antimicrobial resistance under selection.
2. Evolutionary mismatch scoring.
3. Life-history allocation trade-offs.
4. Somatic clonal expansion.
5. Defense-threshold concepts.
6. SQL-backed evolutionary medicine metadata.
7. Cross-language numerical reproducibility.
8. Responsible interpretation of evolutionary explanations.

These examples are educational and methodological scaffolds. They are not clinical prediction systems, treatment tools, public-health forecasts, antimicrobial stewardship systems, cancer models, or diagnostic decision aids. Real applications require clinical evidence, biological validation, epidemiological data, expert review, governance, and appropriate institutional oversight.
