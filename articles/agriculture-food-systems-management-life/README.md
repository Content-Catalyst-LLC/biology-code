# Agriculture, Food Systems, and the Management of Life

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

Agriculture, Food Systems, and the Management of Life

The repository provides reproducible computational scaffolding for agriculture and food-systems analysis. It includes synthetic production-system records, yield and water-productivity calculations, nutrient-use efficiency, soil organic carbon change, biodiversity-resilience scoring, diet-diversity indicators, food-loss accounting, SQL provenance structures, notebook scaffolding, and cross-language examples in Python, R, Julia, Fortran, Rust, Go, C, and C++.

No university affiliation is implied by this repository. The design goal is advanced reproducible computational rigor: clear assumptions, transparent food-system indicators, biological and ecological interpretation, provenance records, responsible-use notes, and reusable scientific workflow structure.

## Repository Structure

- python/ — food-system indicators, biodiversity resilience, soil carbon, diet diversity, food-loss summaries, provenance, and reporting
- r/ — soil organic carbon, diet diversity, and nutrient-flow cross-checks
- julia/ — agricultural indicator numerical kernels
- fortran/ — legacy-style yield and water-productivity kernel
- rust/ — safe command-line resilience-ranking utility
- go/ — portable food-system metadata summary helper
- c/ — compact nutrient-use efficiency kernel
- cpp/ — food-system indicator ranking example
- sql/ — production systems, nutrient flows, soil carbon, diet access, biodiversity, provenance, and notes
- docs/ — setup, methodology, validation, responsible use, and reproducibility notes
- data/ — synthetic agriculture and food-system datasets
- notebooks/ — minimal notebook scaffold
- outputs/ — generated tables, reports, manifests, and figures

## Scientific Focus

The workflows demonstrate:

1. Crop yield and water productivity.
2. Nutrient use efficiency.
3. Food-loss rate.
4. Soil organic carbon change.
5. Biodiversity-resilience scoring.
6. Diet-diversity indicators.
7. SQL-backed food-system metadata.
8. Cross-language numerical reproducibility.
9. Responsible interpretation of agriculture and food-system indicators.

These examples are educational and methodological scaffolds. They are not food-security forecasts, farm-management prescriptions, nutrition diagnostics, climate-risk assessments, regulatory systems, or policy decision tools. Real applications require empirical data, local ecological context, community participation, farmer knowledge, public-health expertise, and appropriate governance.
