# Microbiology and the Hidden Majority of Life

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

**Microbiology and the Hidden Majority of Life**

The code distribution expands the article's compact R and Python examples into a fuller computational microbiology workflow. It includes exponential growth, logistic growth, Monod kinetics, substrate-limited growth, environmental modifiers, microbial condition indexing, community recovery after disturbance, Monte Carlo uncertainty screening, SQL provenance structures, reproducible data files, and multi-language scientific-computing scaffolding.

## Repository Structure

- `python/` — Monod growth, uncertainty screening, recovery modeling, and microbial condition scoring
- `r/` — logistic growth, environmental scenarios, treatment screening, and condition indexing
- `julia/` — substrate-limited growth and community recovery modeling
- `fortran/` — compact logistic and Monod numerical kernels
- `rust/` — safe command-line microbial condition scoring utility
- `go/` — portable microbial recovery scoring helper
- `c/` — logistic and Monod computational kernel
- `cpp/` — comparative microbial scenario simulation
- `sql/` — microbial scenarios, environments, interventions, outputs, and provenance schema
- `docs/` — setup, methodology, and reproducibility notes
- `data/` — small reproducible example datasets
- `notebooks/` — notebook scaffold and workflow notes

## Scientific Focus

The workflows are designed to support transparent microbiology reasoning:

1. Compare microbial growth across environmental scenarios.
2. Model substrate-limited growth using Monod kinetics.
3. Simulate community recovery after disturbance and intervention.
4. Screen microbial condition for restoration or water-quality contexts.
5. Perform Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis.
6. Track data provenance and reproducibility.

These examples are educational scaffolds, not operational clinical, fermentation, wastewater, or ecosystem models. Real applications require calibrated data, microbial measurements, strain or community validation, uncertainty analysis, site context, and expert interpretation.
