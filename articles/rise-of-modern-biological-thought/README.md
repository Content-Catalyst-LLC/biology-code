# The Rise of Modern Biological Thought

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

**The Rise of Modern Biological Thought**

The code distribution expands the article's compact R and Python examples into a reproducible computational workflow for biological intellectual history, quantitative biology, population genetics, growth modeling, sequence comparison, and molecular-information scaffolding.

No university affiliation is implied by this repository. The design goal is advanced reproducible computational rigor: clear assumptions, modular code, transparent data structures, provenance, validation checks, and reproducible workflows.

## Repository Structure

- `python/` — growth models, Hardy-Weinberg expectations, allele-frequency change, sequence similarity, timeline summaries, and validation
- `r/` — growth fitting, Hardy-Weinberg expectations, selection recurrence, and historical milestone summaries
- `julia/` — growth, genotype-frequency, allele-change, and sequence-similarity calculations
- `fortran/` — compact numerical kernel for growth and population-genetics calculations
- `rust/` — safe command-line Hardy-Weinberg and allele-frequency utility
- `go/` — portable genotype-frequency helper
- `c/` — compact modern-biology numerical kernel
- `cpp/` — comparative biological-thought scenario simulation
- `sql/` — historical milestones, growth examples, genotype cases, sequence records, outputs, and provenance schema
- `docs/` — setup, methodology, validation, and reproducibility notes
- `data/` — small reproducible example datasets
- `notebooks/` — notebook scaffold and workflow notes

## Scientific Focus

The workflows are designed to support transparent quantitative treatment of ideas central to modern biological thought:

1. Estimate growth rates and doubling times.
2. Simulate logistic growth under constraint.
3. Calculate Hardy-Weinberg genotype expectations.
4. Simulate allele-frequency change under selection.
5. Compare molecular sequences through simple similarity scoring.
6. Track historical milestones and conceptual transitions.
7. Record provenance, assumptions, and limitations.

These examples are educational and methodological scaffolds. They are not substitutes for expert historical scholarship, population-genetic analysis, phylogenetic inference, clinical genetics, regulatory biotechnology, or production-grade bioinformatics systems.
