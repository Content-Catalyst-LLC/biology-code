# Biotechnology, Intervention, and the Power to Alter Life

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

Biotechnology, Intervention, and the Power to Alter Life

The repository provides reproducible computational scaffolding for biotechnology intervention assessment. It includes synthetic intervention scenarios, risk-benefit scoring, containment-layer logic, equity-adjusted access summaries, ecological release scenarios, SQL provenance structures, notebook scaffolding, and cross-language examples in Python, R, Julia, Fortran, Rust, Go, C, and C++.

No university affiliation is implied by this repository. The design goal is advanced reproducible computational rigor: clear assumptions, transparent intervention scoring, biosafety-aware documentation, governance metadata, provenance records, and reusable scientific workflow structure.

## Repository Structure

- python/ — intervention scoring, containment analysis, equity summaries, provenance, and report generation
- r/ — equity-adjusted access and intervention scenario cross-checks
- julia/ — numerical biotechnology risk-benefit kernel
- fortran/ — legacy-style risk score kernel
- rust/ — safe command-line intervention ranking utility
- go/ — portable biosafety metadata summary helper
- c/ — compact containment probability kernel
- cpp/ — scenario ranking and intervention assessment example
- sql/ — biotechnology interventions, risk dimensions, containment layers, governance notes, and provenance schema
- docs/ — setup, methodology, validation, responsible use, biosafety and biosecurity notes
- data/ — synthetic biotechnology intervention datasets
- notebooks/ — minimal notebook scaffold
- outputs/ — generated tables, reports, manifests, and figures

## Scientific Focus

The workflows demonstrate:

1. Synthetic biotechnology intervention metadata.
2. Benefit, harm, uncertainty, reversibility, and equity dimensions.
3. Containment-layer failure probability.
4. Ecological release scenario scoring.
5. Equity-adjusted access calculations.
6. Biosafety and biosecurity documentation.
7. SQL-backed provenance and governance metadata.
8. Cross-language numerical reproducibility.
9. Responsible interpretation of intervention models.

These examples are educational and methodological scaffolds. They are not regulatory decision systems, clinical assessment tools, biosafety approvals, ecological release models, or biosecurity screening systems. Real biotechnology decisions require domain-specific validation, institutional review, legal compliance, community engagement, ecological assessment, clinical evidence where relevant, and appropriate oversight.
