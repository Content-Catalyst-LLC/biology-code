# Animal Biology and the Organization of Complex Life

This article repository supports the Biology knowledge-series article:

**Animal Biology and the Organization of Complex Life**

The code distribution expands the article's compact R and Python examples into a fuller computational animal-biology workflow. It includes allometric scaling, metabolic demand, trait-based energetic screening, logistic population recovery, survival and hazard screening, stage-structured population projection, intervention comparison, SQL provenance structures, reproducible data files, and multi-language scientific-computing scaffolding.

## Repository Structure

- `python/` — allometry, energy demand, survival screening, stage projection, and population recovery
- `r/` — allometric trait screening and conservation intervention comparison
- `julia/` — allometry, survival, and population projection modeling
- `fortran/` — compact animal population and allometry numerical kernels
- `rust/` — safe command-line animal condition scoring utility
- `go/` — portable animal recovery scoring helper
- `c/` — allometry and survival computational kernel
- `cpp/` — comparative animal scenario simulation
- `sql/` — species traits, population scenarios, survival scenarios, outputs, and provenance schema
- `docs/` — setup, methodology, and reproducibility notes
- `data/` — small reproducible example datasets
- `notebooks/` — notebook scaffold and workflow notes

## Scientific Focus

The workflows are designed to support transparent animal-biology reasoning:

1. Compare allometric metabolic demand across animals of different size.
2. Screen mass-specific energetic demand and exposure risk.
3. Simulate population recovery under intervention scenarios.
4. Compare survival under environmental stress and disease burden.
5. Project juvenile-adult population structure through time.
6. Track data provenance and reproducibility.

These examples are educational scaffolds, not operational wildlife-management, clinical, or conservation models. Real applications require calibrated data, demographic estimates, field measurements, uncertainty analysis, site context, and expert interpretation.
